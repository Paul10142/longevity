The short answer

1. The duplicate check misses about half of real duplicates. Nine episodes are in the library twice (YouTube captions, then a Deepgram transcript). Only 50% of the second copy's facts merged into the first. 42% of the misses sit just under the 0.80 similarity gate, in a band the engine never looks at.
2. Every AI call carries 13× the context it needs. Each call runs as a full Claude Code session with hooks, plugins and tool definitions. One dedup judgement: 52,000 tokens and 8.6 s. Stripped down: 4,000 tokens and 3.3 s, same verdict. This is what kept hitting the subscription's usage limits overnight.
3. The plan ingests everything before producing a single new-style article. The physician reference and protocols, the reason the engine exists, have never been generated by the engine the plan is built around.
The August "database timeout" was not the database. The database never recorded a pipeline statement over 8 seconds. The run was starved by the AI-call path; the two timeouts at the end were the last symptom.

Recommendation in one sentence: fix the call path and the vector storage this week (small, no spend), reorder the plan so the caption redo comes before any re-sweep and before the review push, pull the new synthesis forward onto the 92 clean sources, and decide whether to fund the API for the bulk work (about $60–370 all-in) or keep the laptop as the only engine.

What actually stopped the August run

Two errors were recorded on 28 August, both the app's own 30-second give-up plus one dropped connection. A real database timeout reads differently and none was recorded. The database has run without restart since 30 July. Its statement statistics show slow tails, not failures:

StatementCallsTypicalWorst

Rewrite a claim after a merge2,9180.33 s2.5 s
Recount every claim before an extraction5111.2 s7.8 s

The slow tails come from size: the vector index and the vectors themselves (about 800 MB) no longer fit in a 224 MB cache, and every merge rewrites the index entry for a vector that did not change. That grows with the corpus and must be fixed before the big ingestion, but it is not what produced two claims an hour.

The throughput tells the real story. On 27 August consolidation ran at 8–56 facts per minute all day. From 21:00 it fell to 13, 11, 15, 4, 2, 1, 2 claims per hour, then nothing from 04:00 to 10:00. Extraction created zero facts for 13 hours and restarted one source from scratch three times. Only the AI-call path can produce that shape. The run's log was not kept, so the exact failure that night is unrecorded.

Two code behaviours made it worse: a failed similarity search is silently treated as "no duplicates", so the fact becomes a new claim unchecked; and extraction runs 16 chunks as one all-or-nothing batch, so one failed call throws away fifteen good ones.

Asking the judge about the excluded band

Today the production judge was shown 31 pairs from the 0.75–0.80 band (read-only; nothing merged). 26 of 31 (84%) are the same fact, 5 are different, none unsure, and the result is flat across the band (15 of 17 at 0.750–0.775, 11 of 14 at 0.775–0.800). The five "different" verdicts read correctly; one is a 10-year versus 5-year colonoscopy interval, a contradiction to surface rather than merge. With 27% of claims sitting in this band, roughly a fifth of the library is un-merged duplicates kept apart by the gate alone. This reverses August's "do not lower the threshold", which rested on pairs at 0.82–0.87 and a sweep at 0.86, a different band.

What to change

  • Gate at 0.75 for ingestion and for the corpus sweep. The sweep runs at 0.86 today, stricter than ingestion, which is why August's sweep found 14 merges in 11,502 claims.
  • Show the judge 8–10 candidates instead of 5.
  • A failed search is a failure, never "no duplicates".
  • Refresh a claim's vector after its wording is rewritten; today a six-member claim is still found by its first member's vector.
  • Move vectors out of the hot claim row (or store them half-size) so merges stop rewriting the index and it fits in cache. Upgrade the database tier ($15/month) only if still needed.

The caption redo is bigger and simpler than the notes say

  • 243 caption sources exist. 122 are "unlinked" to the podcast feed, but 102 of them have a pending feed row for the same episode: duplicate rows, created because the linking step keyed on a field these rows never had. The truly YouTube-only set is 4 videos.
  • 20 caption sources already have a Deepgram twin; 9 are extracted twice today. Their caption claims are already superseded.
  • So the redo is: transcribe the remaining 329 feed episodes, extract them, retire the caption claims. No separate YouTube-audio path is needed.

Retirement needs three rules the notes lack. Removing caption members retires the 16,850 caption-only claims (fine). The 1,318 mixed claims had their wording rewritten to include the caption member's detail, so after removal the text may say something no surviving source says; they must be re-derived from survivors. And a claim's vector is its first member's, so where that member goes, re-seed from a survivor. Hold the 213 merge reviews until after: 117 of them involve claims this retires.

Deepgram cost: 329 episodes, 490–590 hours, $165–200 at $0.34/hour. About $141 of credit remains.

Proposed order of work

The 10-03 order (fix timeout → lower gate and re-sweep everything → caption redo → Huberman) re-sweeps 16,850 claims that the next step retires, and produces no article until all of it is done. Proposed instead:

A · this week · no spend

Make the engine safe to run

The CLI call fix; logs kept on disk; failed searches fail loudly; per-chunk retry; vector storage change; gate 0.75 on both paths with a larger candidate list. Prove it on the 18 Deepgram sources already transcribed and waiting.

B · background

Ingestion

Transcribe and extract the 329 feed episodes; retire caption claims under the rules above; one sweep at the new gate over a corpus that is all good transcripts.

C · in parallel with B

The product

File the clean claims into topics; build the sentence-level synthesis from the v4 spec on the 92 good sources; get 3–5 clinician articles and protocols through the gate; you read them as the reviewing physician. This checkpoint has never happened.

D · only after C passes

Huberman and further feeds, then the full build

Design verdict

Keep: the layered model (immutable facts, re-derivable claims, reversible merges, member-level provenance); the checkpointed job queue; enrich-merge with its fidelity guard; speaker-attributed Deepgram transcripts; the human-owned topic spine; the paging discipline; shadow-mode fidelity flags; the visibility gate on thin topics.

Change: the dedup items above; the retirement rules; the call path; batch-level retry in extraction; supervisor logging; the sweep gate.

Defer, so nobody builds them early: corroboration counting (needs per-episode speaker ids first); the experts table; consensus labels; Huberman and other feeds; the mobile review inbox; the taxonomy maintenance job.

Still unmeasured: the extraction-fidelity judge failed certification and the 9 re-checks are still yours. Until a judge is certified, the synthesis gate in C is the only check between an invented detail and a physician.
